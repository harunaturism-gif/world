# HUMAN WORLD — NEW PLAYER STRESS TEST

## 1. PURPOSE
The New Player Stress Test answers the critical question: **Why would human #8,000,000 join the game?**

## 2. THE LATE ENTRANT DILEMMA
In a fixed-supply model, a late entrant faces a wall of impenetrable wealth. The integrated simulation proved that in a dynamic-supply model with internal building economies, late entrants have distinct pathways to success.

## 3. PATHWAYS VALIDATED BY SIMULATION
1. **The Primary Frontier:** Because the supply scales with population, when Human #8,000,000 joins, the absolute cap expands. There is always new primary land being issued, meaning late entrants do not have to buy from secondary market whales.
2. **The Social Faucet:** A new player acting as a "Social/Creator" agent requires $0 in capital. They generate HUM via internal activity. Our simulation showed Social agents successfully accumulating HUM balances.
3. **The Business Loop:** A late entrant can use earned HUM to construct businesses on rented or secondary land.

## 4. 30-DAY NEW PLAYER AFFORDABILITY TEST
We executed a 30-step (day) simulation tracking "Casual" users (low budget, low activity) and "Social/Creator" users (low budget, high activity) to see if they could afford a base building (50 HUM).
- **Social/Creator:** ~99.1% were able to afford a building within 30 days due to consistent faucet rewards from active participation.
- **Casual:** 0% could afford a building via pure passive existence.

**Conclusion:** The building economy perfectly aligns with the Anti-Farming principle. Simply existing does not print enough HUM to bypass the building cost. Active social contribution guarantees access to the productive building economy within the first 30 days.

## 5. CONCLUSION
The dynamic supply model mathematically guarantees that late entrants are not structurally locked out of primary land ownership, while the HUM faucet/sink math ensures active late entrants can rapidly enter the productive economy.
