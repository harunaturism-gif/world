# HUMAN WORLD — ECONOMIC CONSTITUTION v0.1

Purpose:
Define the economic foundations of Human World before production development begins.

Core thesis:
A persistent virtual world where verified humans own scarce, uniquely identified land, develop property, participate in markets and social activity, and use an internal economic system.

Current hypotheses to validate:
- Land has a finite maximum supply.
- Plot IDs are permanent and unique.
- World ID / proof of human is used to establish unique human citizenship/ownership.
- HUM is an internal unit of account; WLD is external.
- Land value should be discovered through demand/activity/development rather than guaranteed appreciation.
- A secondary market, auctions, offers/trades and development should create economic activity.
- Buildings, furniture, businesses and other assets can add utility to land.
- Social functionality can connect people, properties, offers and trades.
- Tokenomics should be designed only after the economy proves that a token is genuinely useful.

Important unresolved questions:
1. How many plots should exist?
2. How should initial land supply relate to human population?
3. How should primary land pricing work?
4. How should secondary-market prices be discovered?
5. What exactly is HUM and how is it issued?
6. How do we prevent inflation, deflation spirals and economic death?
7. How do we prevent whales from dominating the world?
8. How do new users enter when land becomes scarce?
9. How does land acquire utility beyond speculation?
10. How do buildings create real economic activity?
11. How do auctions and peer-to-peer offers work?
12. What should protocol fees fund?
13. What should the token allocation and vesting look like?
14. What legal/regulatory constraints apply?
15. Can the entire economy be simulated before implementation?

Research foundations:
- Karl Marx — commodities, use value/exchange value, capital, accumulation
- Adam Smith — division of labor, specialization, market prices
- Friedrich Hayek — decentralized price information
- Joseph Schumpeter — entrepreneurship, innovation, creative destruction
- John Maynard Keynes — demand, expectations, economic cycles
- Milton Friedman — markets, property, competition
- Bitcoin — predictable issuance, scarcity, credible rules, monetary network effects

Simulation requirement:
Model at minimum 100,000; 1,000,000; and 10,000,000 humans.

Stress tests:
- WLD price -50%
- population +100%
- land demand +300%
- whale acquisition
- mass inactivity
- mass migration
- speculative bubble
- market crash
- new technology
- new district

Do not assume the hypotheses are correct. The objective is to falsify weak economic designs before significant development.
