# HUMAN WORLD — COMPETITOR ANALYSIS v1.0

## 1. PURPOSE
This document evaluates existing virtual economies and land systems to ensure Human World does not repeat their mistakes. For each major mechanic, we ask: "Does this already exist?" and "What makes our approach different and sustainable?"

## 2. VIRTUAL WORLD COMPETITORS

### A. Decentraland (MANA)
**Mechanics:**
- **Land Supply:** Fixed absolute supply (90,601 LAND parcels).
- **Pricing/Market:** Highly speculative, priced in MANA.
- **Result:** Extreme concentration of wealth, vast amounts of "dead" or empty land, and prohibitive entry costs for new players.
**Human World Difference:**
We reject the fixed absolute supply model precisely because of the Decentraland failure mode. Scarcity without utility creates ghost towns. Our land must have use-value, and our supply model must scale with the verified human population.

### B. The Sandbox (SAND)
**Mechanics:**
- **Land Supply:** Fixed absolute supply (166,464 LANDs).
- **Economy:** Creator economy focus; highly financialized with staking and speculative land sales.
- **Result:** Similar to Decentraland—land acts primarily as a speculative asset rather than a productive one. High barrier to entry.
**Human World Difference:**
We will test dynamic/activity-linked land issuance. Furthermore, the Human World economy will not rely on staking yields or inflationary token rewards to create artificial demand.

### C. Upland
**Mechanics:**
- **Land System:** Mapped to real-world addresses.
- **Economy:** Internal fiat-pegged currency (UPX) for minting properties; properties generate passive UPX yield.
- **Result:** Inflationary loop where simply owning land generates currency, requiring constant new-player capital or aggressive sinks to balance.
**Human World Difference:**
Human World land will not generate passive yield simply by existing. Economic returns must stem from actual utility (development, human dwell time, productive businesses).

### D. Atlas Earth
**Mechanics:**
- **Land System:** Mapped to the real world; players buy tiny parcels.
- **Economy:** Parcels generate micro-fractions of actual fiat rent per second.
- **Result:** Essentially a gamified ad-viewing platform and pseudo-investment scheme with massive inflation and fractional payouts.
**Human World Difference:**
Human World fundamentally rejects the "promise of appreciation/yield" model. WLD or HUM will not be distributed simply for holding land.

### E. EVE Online
**Mechanics:**
- **Economy:** Highly sophisticated, player-driven economy with strict faucet/sink management (Plex, ISK).
- **Production:** Almost all assets are player-manufactured.
- **Result:** One of the most stable and long-lasting virtual economies in history.
**Human World Difference:**
We will adopt EVE's rigorous macroeconomic management—specifically the strict monitoring of faucets vs. sinks, and the principle of destruction/decay to prevent asset bloat. EVE is our primary economic inspiration, applied to a spatial/land paradigm.

### F. Second Life
**Mechanics:**
- **Economy:** Linden Dollars (L$) with a robust creator economy and land rental system.
- **Land System:** Users rent server space (land) from the developer, which acts as an ongoing maintenance fee (sink).
- **Result:** A highly durable, mostly stable fiat-pegged economy driven by genuine social utility and creator labor.
**Human World Difference:**
Second Life proves that social utility and creator economies work. However, Human World leverages World ID for unique human verification, allowing for democratic ownership structures and Sybil-resistant distribution mechanisms that Second Life cannot support.

### G. Axie Infinity
**Mechanics:**
- **Economy:** Dual-token model (AXS/SLP).
- **Result:** Classic inflationary collapse. High initial yields drew massive player influx, but when new player entry slowed, the asset prices crashed because there was no external/intrinsic sink for the generated tokens.
**Human World Difference:**
Human World is subjected to a "No-Ponzi" test. The economy must not depend on perpetual new-user money to pay existing users. We will model economic velocity, not just issuance.

### H. World Mini Apps / Other World Ecosystem Land Projects
**Mechanics:**
- **Current State:** The World ecosystem is nascent. Some projects may attempt simple "claim your plot with World ID" mechanics.
- **Result:** If they rely only on the novelty of World ID and fixed scarcity, they will face the Decentraland problem.
**Human World Difference:**
Human World uses World ID not just as a login, but as the foundation for its wealth control mechanisms (e.g., human-level acquisition caps) and as a metric for genuine utility (e.g., measuring unique human dwell time to determine land value without botting).

## 3. CORE DESIGN TAKEAWAYS
1. **Utility Over Speculation:** If land is just a token on a map, the project will fail. Land must host productive assets (buildings, businesses).
2. **Sink-Driven Economy:** Passive yields are fatal. Faucets must be tied to labor/utility, and sinks (maintenance, construction, fees) must rigorously remove excess currency.
3. **Dynamic Supply:** Fixed scarcity creates exclusionary oligarchies. Supply must dynamically expand based on population and economic activity to ensure late entrants have a viable path.
4. **Anti-Sybil Leverage:** World ID is our unique moat. We must use it to enforce anti-concentration rules that other crypto games structurally cannot enforce.
