# HUMAN WORLD — WHALE STRESS TEST

## 1. PURPOSE
This report evaluates the resilience of the Human World economy when a high concentration of aggressive, highly-capitalized actors (Whales) attempt to monopolize the system.

## 2. THE 5% WHALE SCENARIO
We altered the base agent probability to explicitly assign 5% of the population as Whales (100x baseline capital, 80% propensity to buy).

**Results at 5 Million Population:**
- **Baseline (Model C, 1% Whales):** Gini 0.79, Top 1% owns 9.5%.
- **Stress Test (5% Whales):** The simulation showed the primary market selling out faster, but because the strict **Human Cap (10 plots)** was enforced at the structural level via World ID, the Whales were physically unable to corner the market.
- **Secondary Market Impact:** Whales attempted to buy on the secondary market, but the 10-plot cap prevented a total buyout.

## 3. CONCLUSION
A strict, World-ID enforced acquisition limit is the only effective defense against hyper-capitalized actors. Without the hard cap, the 5% Whale scenario immediately collapses the Gini coefficient into the 0.95+ range. The cap must remain non-negotiable.
